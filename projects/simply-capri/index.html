6 Ways To Tell A Real Italian Restaurant From One That Just Says It Is

Every Italian place in Orlando uses the word authentic, which is why it stopped meaning anything, and the only way left to tell them apart is to check six specific things that cost real money to fake.

Dining · Orlando & Horizon West 7 min read

I've eaten at a lot of restaurants around Orlando that describe themselves as "authentic Italian."

Most of them mean the sauce is red.

The word has been used so many times around here that it lost its meaning.

So here's a more useful question than "is it authentic."

What would a restaurant have to actually do, at real expense, to be Italian instead of Italian-themed?

Six things. All six are checkable. All six are sitting inside one building in Flamingo Crossings, five minutes from Disney's western gate.

01People

The Staff Not Only Speak Italian, They Are Italians.

Look at a server's name pin at Simply Capri. Under the name, there's a city. Torino. Salerno. Napoli.

They're not for show. The restaurant runs a cultural exchange program and brings its people over from Italy.

Somebody with a "funny" accent who is genuinely happy you came comes over, asks if it's your first time, tells stories about his home town in Italy, and has excellent dish recommendations.

Nearly every person that dines here says it:

02Equipment

The oven came from Naples. On a boat.

This is the part that's genuinely hard to fake, because it costs a fortune and you can't hide it in the kitchen.

The oven at Simply Capri is a hand-built Acunto, made in Naples, shipped to Florida, and reassembled on site.

It runs around 800 degrees. That means your pizza is in and out in roughly ninety seconds, which is the only way you get that blistered, leopard-spotted underside on a crunchy Neapolitan crust.

03Geography

It's not "Italian." It's about thirty miles of Italian coastline.

Here's the tell that most people miss.

Italy isn't a cuisine. It's twenty regions that have been arguing about food for six hundred years.

That's why Simply Capri picked a coastline and stayed on it. Capri, Sorrento, the Amalfi Coast, Naples.

Each dish is from a different town along the coastline:

The delicious, zesty Tagliatelle alla Nerano is from a village of about four hundred people on the Sorrentine peninsula.

Then the mouth-watering Scialatielli dei Faraglioni is named after the rock formations off Capri. Then there's the Pizza Anacapri, Insalata di Capri, Scampi Amalfitani, and so on.

Those aren't dish names you invent in a branding meeting in Orlando. Those are the names of places somebody actually misses.

"The taste is like in Italy, not Americanized like other restaurants." — spinnea, TripAdvisor
04Sourcing

The lemon cake is from a bakery in a town of three thousand people.

The Delizia al Limone on the dessert menu is a limoncello sponge cake wrapped in lemon cream. It's made to the recipe from Pasticceria Sal De Riso in Minori, which is a small town on the Amalfi Coast where that cake is a minor religion.

06Honest concession

The plates are smaller than you expect. That's on purpose, and it changes how you order.

If you like eating a lot of the same thing, this might not be for you.

If you come in expecting an American-Italian portion, the mountain of penne with a ladle of cream sauce, you will look at your plate and think it's small. Some people have said exactly that in reviews, and they're not wrong about what they saw.

But that's what Southern Italian food is.

Simple, modest, and delicious.

A Margherita at Simply Capri has rich, sweet San Marzano tomatoes, mozzarella, Parmigiano and basil on it.

That's all it takes for such a praised, crunchy, authentic pizza, steamy from an imported oven.

"The pepperoni pizza was delicious, just enough for two for a late lunch." — Margaret C., Yelp

So order the way an Italian family would. Get the antipasto board for the table. Share a pizza. Split a pasta. Get one of the fish. Order dessert like you mean it.

Do it that way and you'll be at the table for two hours and leave full. Order it like a chain restaurant and you'll be disappointed by both the portion and the check.
