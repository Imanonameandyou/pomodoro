Rules

Do

  • Track all-caps type wide — 0.13em minimum. Tight caps break the wordmark's family resemblance.
  • Keep display type to one idea per screen. Two competing serif headlines is one too many.
  • Set body copy at Light. Regular reads heavy against the blue.
  • Use sentence case for paragraphs and headlines; reserve caps for navigation and buttons.

Don't

  • No script or handwriting faces. Simply Capri is not a trattoria pastiche.
  • No italic display type. It softens the Roman geometry.
  • No bold weights above 500 anywhere in the system.
  • No text set over the busy center of a pizza photograph — use the blue panel instead.

Marchio

The lockup

A short rule, "simply" in lowercase light, then CAPRI in tracked capitals. The rule is structural, not decorative — it sets the left margin the two words hang from.

simply CAPRI
Clear space = cap height of "CAPRI" on all four sides

Approved

  • Capri Blue on white or Bianco Capri.
  • White on Capri Blue.
  • White on photography, only where the underlying image is dark and even.
  • Minimum width: 90px digital, 1 inch print.

Never

  • Recolored to any extended-palette tone.
  • Stretched, condensed, or re-tracked.
  • Given a drop shadow, outline, or gradient.
  • Set inside a box when it already sits on a blue field.
  • Placed on a busy photograph without a scrim.

Immagine

Shot close, lit warm, cropped tight

Photography is the second half of the identity. The blue is cool and flat on purpose — the food supplies every bit of warmth in the brand.

Direction

  • Warm daylight or oven light. No cool white, no flash.
  • Crop past the edge of the plate. Full table scenes read as stock.
  • Show the char, the leopard spotting, the oil pooling. Imperfection is the proof.
  • Wood, linen, and white ceramic as surfaces — nothing painted, nothing branded.
  • People appear mid-meal and mid-conversation, never posed with a fork raised.
  • The exterior under palms and blue sky is the establishing shot. Use it once per campaign, not every post.

Avoid

  • Heavy filters, teal-and-orange grading, or crushed blacks.
  • Overhead flat-lays of six dishes arranged in a grid.
  • Any AI-generated food where the cheese pull defies gravity or basil grows from the crust.
  • Text burned across the middle of the dish.
  • Red-checkered cloth, Leaning Tower props, accordion clip art.

Voce

Warm host, not a menu description

The restaurant's own words already set the register: plain, confident, a little Italian, never precious. Short sentences. No superlatives it can't back up.

Tone rules

Sounds like us

  • "Wood-fired, 90 seconds, out to your table."
  • Italian used for real things — Amalfi, Capri, benvenuti — not sprinkled for flavor.
  • Specific over grand: "house-made pastas" beats "culinary excellence."
  • Invitations, not commands. "Come celebrate" over "Book now or miss out."

Doesn't

  • "Authentic taste of Italy" and every phrase like it.
  • Exclamation points stacked on discounts.
  • Mamma-mia mimicry or fake accents in captions.
  • Chef-speak the guest would have to look up.
